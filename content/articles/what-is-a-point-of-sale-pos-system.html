If you run a shop, you have probably heard the words "point of sale" or "POS".

It sounds technical, but the idea is simple: a point of sale system helps you make a sale, record it and give your customer a receipt.

This guide explains what a point of sale (POS) system is, what it records, what you need to use one, and what to look for when choosing a POS for a small shop.

What Is a Point of Sale (POS) System?

The point of sale is the place and the moment where a customer pays you for a product or service. In a shop, it is the counter.

A point of sale (POS) system is the tool you use at that moment. It records what was sold, works out the total, records how the customer paid and produces a receipt.

Today most POS systems are software, an app on a phone or computer, instead of a paper notebook or a mechanical till.

What Does a POS System Record?

Every time you make a sale, a POS system can keep a record of:

  • What was sold and the quantity
  • The selling price and the total
  • Whether the sale was cash or credit
  • The customer, when relevant
  • The date and time
  • The employee who handled the sale

Because the record is made at the moment of the sale, you do not have to rely on memory at the end of the day.

Why Would a Small Shop Use a POS?

A notebook can work for a very small business. As the number of products, customers and daily sales grows, a POS can help you:

  • Keep every sale organized in one place
  • Give customers a clear receipt
  • Connect sales with your stock records
  • Know who bought on credit and how much they still owe
  • Review your sales by day, week or month
  • Control what your employees can see and do

How a Sale Works in a POS

The steps are usually the same, whatever system you use:

  1. Choose the products. Select what the customer is buying and the quantity.
  2. Check the total. The system adds up the items for you.
  3. Choose the customer, if needed. This matters most for credit sales.
  4. Record how the customer pays. For example, a cash sale paid in full, or a credit sale that will be paid later.
  5. Give the receipt. Print it, send it by phone or save it as a file.
  6. Let the records update. In a system connected to your inventory, the sale also reduces the stock quantity.

Cash Sales and Credit Sales

Many small shops sell both for cash and on credit.

A cash sale is paid in full at the time of the sale.

A credit sale is still a sale, but the customer has not paid yet. The shop needs to record the sale and also the amount the customer owes, so that repayments can be tracked later.

A good POS keeps these two kinds of sale separate, so you can see how much money you have actually received and how much customers still owe you.

What Do You Need to Use a POS?

You do not necessarily need expensive equipment. In general you need:

  • The POS software, such as an app
  • A device to run it, such as a phone or a computer
  • Your product list, with prices

Some shops also use a thermal receipt printer. It is useful if you want paper receipts, but it is not the only way to give a receipt: many businesses also send receipts through messaging apps or save them as PDF files.

What to Look for in a POS for a Small Shop

Before you choose a POS, ask yourself:

  • Does it work without internet? If your connection is unreliable, you need to be able to keep selling.
  • Is it simple? You and your employees should be able to use it without long training.
  • Does it run on devices you already have? A phone or a computer you already own keeps the cost down.
  • How can you give receipts? Printing, WhatsApp and PDF are all useful options.
  • Can it handle credit sales and customer debts?
  • Does it connect with your stock?
  • Can you control employee access?
  • Can you try it first? A free trial lets you test it with your own business.

Common Mistakes When Starting With a POS

1. Entering products with missing prices

If prices or costs are missing or wrong, your totals and your profit information will be wrong too.

2. Not recording credit sales

A credit sale that is not recorded is easy to forget, and the debt becomes hard to follow up.

3. Forgetting to check physical stock

A POS can only show the quantities it has been told about. Count your stock regularly and compare it with the system.

4. Everyone using the same login

When each employee has their own access, you can see who handled each sale.
