What Bizora is

Bizora is an offline-first business management application built for small and growing businesses across Africa. It brings the everyday parts of running a business — sales, customers, credit and debt, inventory, receipts, reports and staff access — into one place, on a device you already own.

The idea is straightforward: the information you need to run your business well shouldn't be scattered across notebooks, phone messages and memory. And it shouldn't stop being available the moment the network does.

The Bizora approach

How we think about building business software for this market.

Start with what actually happens in a shop. Features earn their place by solving something a business owner does every day — recording a sale, checking who owes money, seeing what's left on the shelf — not by adding to a feature list.

Make the important information visible. Sales, customer balances, stock levels and financial performance should be quick to see, not buried behind steps.

Assume the connection will drop. Offline operation isn't a fallback mode bolted on at the end; it's the assumption the product is designed around.

Respect the device. Bizora is built to stay usable across a broad range of mobile hardware, including lower-end Android phones — because that's what most of its users are actually holding.

Why offline-first matters

Bizora is designed so that core business operations can continue without depending on a constant internet connection.

For a business operating where connectivity is inconsistent, software that stops working when the network drops isn't just inconvenient — it interrupts trading. So sales can be recorded, stock stays accurate, and customer balances remain available whether or not you're connected.

To be clear about what that does and doesn't mean: internet is still needed for installing Bizora, receiving updates, contacting support, and any applicable licensing or payment process. Bizora does not currently provide cloud synchronization, automatic cloud backups, or remote data recovery. Your business data lives on your device, and backups are something you create and keep.
